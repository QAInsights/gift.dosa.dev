You hand the card to the cashier and it works. You type the same number into the same store's website and it fails. Nothing is broken. The two checkouts are simply asking for different proof.

## The register asks for less

In a physical store, the scanner or magnetic stripe identifies the card and the till trusts that you hold it. Many registers never ask for the PIN at all. It feels effortless because the store is verifying the object in your hand.

## The website asks for more

Online, nobody can see the card, so the checkout compensates. It typically wants the full number plus the PIN under the scratch panel, and some retailers also want the card registered to a name and billing address before the first online use. Miss any one of those and the payment fails, even with money on the card.

## Some cards are in-store only by design

Merchandise return cards, fitting-room credits, and certain promotional cards are coded for the till. Their terms say "redeemable in-store" in small print that nobody reads until checkout fails. This is a policy, not a bug, and no amount of retyping will fix it. The fix is a trip to the shop, or asking support whether the value can move to an online-capable card.

## The balance gap

Online checkouts are stricter about partial totals. If your order is $52 and the card holds $50, many sites reject the card outright instead of asking for a second payment method. Check the exact balance first, then shape the order to fit it, or confirm the retailer supports a second payment online before you start.

## What to do, in order

First, scratch the PIN panel and enter the full details carefully. Second, look for a gift-card registration or "manage card" page on the retailer's site and attach your name and address. Third, check the card type: if it came from a return, assume in-store only until proven otherwise. Fourth, if it still fails, contact the retailer's support with the card number ready. They can see the decline reason, which is almost never shown to you.

Once it works, record the balance and where you spent it. A card you understand is a card you will actually finish.
