The card is new, the occasion was happy, and the balance reads zero. Before you worry, know the most common cause: the cashier never scanned the activation barcode. It happens, and it is fixable.

## Step one: find the receipt

The purchase receipt is the whole case. It proves the card was bought, when, where, and for how much. If you bought the card, dig it out. If you received it, ask the giver. This conversation feels awkward for about ten seconds and then solves the problem. Without proof of purchase, every later step gets harder.

## Step two: return to the selling store

Take the card and the receipt to the store that sold it. The service desk can verify the purchase and activate the card on the spot. This works best at the exact location of purchase, since their registers hold the transaction. Call ahead if the store is far, and ask what they need you to bring.

## Step three: contact the issuer

If the store cannot help, contact the company behind the card: the retailer for store cards, the network's service line for Visa or Mastercard gift cards. Have the card number, the receipt details, and your ID ready. Many issuers can activate remotely once they verify the purchase. Keep a note of who you spoke to and any case number.

## Check the packaging for tampering

While you wait, look at the card's packaging with fresh eyes. Scratched PIN panels, re-glued packaging, or stickers over barcodes are signs the card may have been tampered with before purchase, a known scam where thieves copy card details and drain the value after activation. If you see tampering, tell the store and the issuer plainly. And going forward, buy cards from behind the counter or from sealed displays, never from a picked-over rack.

## If someone told you to pay with gift cards, stop

No real business or government agency asks for payment in gift cards. The Federal Trade Commission puts it directly: gift cards are for gifts, not for payments, and anyone demanding them is a scammer. If that is your situation, do not scratch the PIN or share the numbers. Report it to the card issuer and to the FTC through their [gift card consumer advice page](https://consumer.ftc.gov/search-terms/gift-cards).
