Most gift cards die with a few dollars still on them. Not because the money is hard to spend, but because nobody plans the last purchase. A little intent fixes that.

## Know the exact balance first

Guessing leads to declined payments and embarrassment at the register. Check the balance through the retailer's own website, app, or printed receipt, then write the number down. If the card lives in your cabinet, update the stored balance and the date you confirmed it.

## Split the payment in-store

This is the most reliable move for small balances. Tell the cashier the exact amount on the card before they total the sale, pay that part with the gift card, and cover the rest with another method. Most point-of-sale systems support this, but policies differ, so asking first saves an awkward reversal.

## Online, check before checkout

Some online checkouts accept a gift card plus a credit card. Others accept only one gift card, or require the card to cover the full total. Look for a second payment field or a gift-card help page before you fill your cart. If the store does not support it, switch to an in-store trip or a smaller order.

## Make the last purchase intentional

The trap is buying something you do not want to "use up" free money. Instead, attach the balance to something already on your list: groceries, fuel, a refill, a bill the retailer sells. The balance disappears and nothing extra enters your home.

## Record what is left

After any partial spend, update the balance and the date immediately. A card with a fresh, trusted number gets used. A card with a stale guess gets ignored until it expires or is forgotten.
