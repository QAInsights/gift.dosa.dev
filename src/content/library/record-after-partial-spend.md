A gift card with $23.17 and today's date on it gets spent. The same card with a two-year-old guess gets left in the drawer until it expires. The difference is never the money. It is the trust in the number.

## The 30-second habit

After every partial spend, before you leave the register or close the tab, record three things: the new balance, the date, and where you spent it. The receipt usually prints the remaining balance, which makes this free in-store. Online, the confirmation page or your account often shows it. Thirty seconds now saves a balance-check errand later.

## Pick one home for records

A gift card app, a phone note, a small envelope with the receipt tucked inside. The system matters less than the consistency. If your household shares cards, keep the shared list somewhere everyone can see, with the store, balance, and holder, and keep the codes themselves private until someone is ready to spend.

## Photograph the proof

Receipts fade and get lost. A quick photo of the receipt showing the remaining balance, kept with the card's record, settles every future argument about what is left. If the card itself has no balance printed anywhere, the photo becomes the card's memory.

## Rescue the mystery cards

Everyone has one: a card with no record and no memory attached. Once or twice a year, gather every mystery card and check each balance through the retailer's own site or app. Record each result with the date. Cards with real value rejoin the plan. Empty ones leave the drawer. Either way the fog lifts.

## Tie it to the spend

Records pay off when they shape the next purchase. When a card drops under $10, give it a mission: the next grocery run, the next refill, the next bill the retailer sells.
