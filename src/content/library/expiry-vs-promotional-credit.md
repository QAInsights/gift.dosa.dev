Two cards, both marked $25, both from the same store. One lasts five years. The other dies next month. They look identical because the difference is legal, not visual.

## Real gift cards: years, not weeks

When someone pays money for a gift card, consumer law in many places protects it. In the United States, the federal CARD Act requires most gift cards to remain usable for at least five years from issue or last reload, and restricts dormancy fees in the meantime. Several Canadian provinces ban expiry dates on most gift cards outright. Other countries vary, so check your local rules, but the pattern holds: paid value gets strong protection.

## Promotional credits: weeks, not years

Bonus cards, "get $10 with purchase" credits, loyalty rewards, and app coupons are not purchased value. They are marketing, and their terms show it: expiry in 30 or 60 days is normal, plus limits on where and how they apply. The store gave them free and priced them to push a quick visit. Treat every deadline on a promo credit as real, because it is.

## How to tell which you hold

Read the receipt or the email, not the card. Paid amount on the receipt means gift card rules. Words like bonus, reward, promotional, or "with purchase" mean promo rules. When a purchase mixes both, a $100 gift card plus a $10 bonus, the two balances live side by side with different clocks. Record both, with both dates.

## The spending order that never fails

Spend promotional credits first, always. They expire sooner, work in fewer places, and vanish without the legal safety net. Then spend down gift cards oldest first. This order sounds obvious, yet most expired value comes from doing the reverse: saving the "free" credit for later while spending the protected card now.

## Put the deadlines somewhere loud

Promo deadlines deserve a calendar reminder a week before they hit, not a mental note. Gift card dates can live quietly with the card's record. If your cabinet tracks expiry and reminders, file promo credits the day they arrive. A bonus you remember in time is a discount. One you remember late is a lesson.
