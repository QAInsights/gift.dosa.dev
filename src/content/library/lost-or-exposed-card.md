Losing a gift card feels worse than it is, and exposing its details is worse than it feels. The lost card might be replaceable. The photographed PIN is an emergency. Here is how to handle each.

## If the card is lost but you have proof

Find the purchase receipt and any record of the card number: a photo, a filed copy, an order email. Contact the issuer, explain the card is lost, and ask whether they can freeze the balance and issue a replacement. Policies vary widely. Some issuers do this routinely with proof, others never do. Your receipt and your record are what make the request possible at all, which is why filing cards when they arrive matters.

## If the card is lost with no proof

Be honest with yourself about the odds. Without a receipt or a card number, the issuer cannot tell your card from any other, and most will decline. Still check: search your email for order confirmations, ask the giver for the receipt, look for photos. Then accept the result and let it change one habit: from now on, every card gets recorded the day it arrives.

## If the number and PIN are exposed

A card number plus PIN is spendable by anyone, from anywhere, in seconds. If you pasted both into a chat, emailed them, or entered them on a shady site, move now: spend the balance through the retailer's real site or app, or load it into your store account if the retailer allows that. Every minute you spend deciding is a minute someone else can spend spending. Afterward, contact the issuer, explain the exposure, and ask about freezing or replacing the card.

## If a scammer has the details

The FTC's rule is absolute: gift cards are for gifts, not for payments, and anyone who told you to pay with one is a scammer. Contact the company that issued the card immediately and ask about stopping the transfer, then report the scam to the FTC through their [gift card consumer advice page](https://consumer.ftc.gov/search-terms/gift-cards). Keep copies of the card, the receipt, and every message. Recovery is uncommon, but reports help investigators connect cases.

## Make the next card boring to lose

File new cards the day they arrive, with the number stored safely and the balance and date recorded. Keep PINs out of chats, emails, and shared notes. Know where your household's list lives and who holds each card. A card with a record is recoverable. A card with only a memory is a hope.
