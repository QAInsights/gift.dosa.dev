Nobody wants three cards with $4 each. The good news: you can usually spend them together. The details depend on where you stand when you pay.

## Splitting at the register

This is the reliable path. Tell the cashier the exact amount on each card before they total the sale, and ask to split the payment. Most point-of-sale systems handle a gift card plus a credit card, or even two gift cards, without drama. The key is asking before the total, because reversing a completed sale to re-split it is where awkwardness lives.

## Splitting online

Online checkouts are pickier. Some accept a gift card plus a credit card. Many accept exactly one gift card and require it to cover the whole order. A few let you stack several. There is no universal rule, so look at the payment page before you build your cart. If the page shows a single gift card field and no second-payment option, plan around one card.

## Merging into a store account

Several large retailers let you load a gift card into your online account balance. This is the cleanest way to combine: three fiddly cards become one number. Not every store offers it, and loaded value sometimes comes with its own terms, so read the account-balance page once. When it exists, use it early rather than hoarding cards.

## When nothing combines

Return cards, single-use promo codes, and cards from different brands will not merge. For those, spend each one deliberately: attach each small balance to something already on your list, in-store where splitting is easiest. Our guide to [using the last few dollars](/library/small-balances) walks through that endgame.

## Keep the record current

Every split or merge changes the numbers. Update each card's balance and the date right after paying, while the receipt is still in your hand. Combined spending only stays simple if the records keep up.
