Every family has the same archaeology: gift card PINs buried in years of chat history, on old phones, in screenshots nobody deleted. It works until it doesn't. A small system replaces all of it.

## The problem with chat

A PIN sent by text lives forever. It sits in backups, on a tablet a kid uses, in a screenshot roll, in a chat group that gained members since. Nobody means to leak it. The medium leaks by design. The fix is not better chat. It is needing chat less.

## The shared list: everything except the code

Keep one list the whole household can see: store, current balance with the date it was confirmed, who holds the physical card or the code, and any expiry. A gift card app, a shared note, a sheet on the fridge. Everyone can answer "do we have anything for the bookstore?" without touching a single credential. Update the balance right after each spend, while the receipt is still warm.

## The handoff: codes at the moment of use

When someone is ready to pay, the holder shares the code directly, ideally in person or by reading it over a call, for immediate use. The code travels once, gets spent, and has no afterlife in chat history. For online spending together, the holder can enter the code themselves or share a screen. Awkward for ten seconds, safe forever.

## One keeper per card

Every card has a named holder who knows where it is and what it holds. Cards without keepers drift into drawers and die there. When the keeper changes, the list changes the same day. Kids old enough to shop can keep their own cards on the list, with a parent able to see the balances. Responsibility scales better than secrecy.

## Give small balances a mission

A household list makes leftovers visible, which is half the battle. When a card drops under $10, assign it: next grocery run, next refill, next bill that retailer sells. Visible small balances with missions get spent. Hidden ones become drawer fossils. Pair this with our [record-keeping habit](/library/record-after-partial-spend) and the list stays trustworthy all year.
