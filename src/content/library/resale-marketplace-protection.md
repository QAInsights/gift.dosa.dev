A gift card from a stranger is a promise from a stranger. The marketplace's buyer protection is what turns that promise into something you can rely on, or reveals that you cannot. Read it before the price.

## How resale deals fail

The common failure is the drained card: the seller, or someone the seller shared the numbers with, spends the balance after you pay. Stolen cards are worse and can be frozen by the issuer even after you buy them. Both failures look identical at checkout, a working-looking number, which is why protection terms matter more than seller ratings.

## The three questions that matter

First, who verifies the balance, and when? The best marketplaces check the balance themselves before listing or before releasing your payment. "Seller states the balance" is not verification. Second, how long is the guarantee window? A year of protection beats 30 days by a mile, because drained cards are often discovered late. Third, are refunds cash or site credit? Credit locks your refund into buying another stranger's card. Cash admits the marketplace stands behind the sale.

## Red flags that end the conversation

Discounts far deeper than every competitor, pressure to pay off-platform, sellers who refuse verification, and terms that promise "support" without naming windows or refund methods. Each one means the marketplace keeps the fee while you keep the risk. Walk away. There will be another listing.

## After you buy: verify, then hurry

Check the balance through the retailer's own site or app the moment you receive the number. Then shorten your exposure: spend the card soon, or load it into your store account where the retailer allows it. A resale balance sitting for months is an invitation to whoever else holds the digits. Save the order confirmation and every message until the value is fully spent.

## The honest alternative

For gift-giving, buy new cards from the retailer. Resale discounts are for your own planned spending, where you can verify fast and spend faster. And remember the FTC's warning in the other direction: if anyone asks you to pay them with gift cards, that is a scam, not a marketplace. Their [gift card consumer advice page](https://consumer.ftc.gov/search-terms/gift-cards) explains how to report it.
