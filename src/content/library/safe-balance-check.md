A gift card number plus its PIN is cash to anyone who holds both. Fake balance-check sites exist for exactly one reason: to collect that pair. Safe checking is mostly about where you type, not what you type.

## Start from the address bar, not search

Type the retailer's official address yourself, or open the retailer's own app. Search results and ads can be bought or faked, and a convincing copy of a balance page costs an attacker almost nothing. The address bar is the only part of the page they cannot dress up.

## Never split your trust

A legitimate balance check happens on the retailer's own property. If a page asks for your card number and PIN but the domain is unfamiliar, close it. No coupon blog, checker tool, or forum needs your full card details to "verify" anything.

## Keep the number and PIN apart

Store the card where you can find it, but treat the number and PIN as the valuable part. Avoid pasting both into chats, emails, or shared notes. If your household shares cards, share the balance and the store, not the credentials, until someone is ready to spend.

## If details were exposed, move fast

Once a number and PIN are out, assume someone else will try them. Spend the balance through the real retailer right away, or ask official support whether the card can be frozen or replaced. Then record what happened so the card in your cabinet reflects reality.

## Make the next check boring

Save the official balance page for each store you actually use, and file new cards the day they arrive. Safe habits beat clever detection: the check you do the same careful way every time is the one that never leaks.
